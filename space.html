The 3D space

On the home page, Cypher lays your store out as a route through a city. Each block of the Cypher space section is a stop; the camera flies from one to the next and lands with the stop centred on screen.

Getting around

  • Stop menu: a carousel of the stops, each with a small picture taken from the stop. Theme settings → Storm → Stop menu position places it on the left (default), under the header, at the bottom or on the right; phones always use the bottom.
  • Keyboard: the left and right arrows and the space bar move between stops; Tab moves through each stop’s links and flies to the stop that holds them.
  • Addresses: each stop has its own address (#stop-3), so links can open on a stop, and the browser’s Back button returns to the previous one.
  • Scrolling scrolls the page as usual; a stop with more than fits on screen (a big batch of products) scrolls inside itself.

3D or page view

The View // 3D / Page switch in the header lets every visitor choose (hovering each option explains it); the choice is remembered in their browser. Page view shows the same stops as a regular scrolling page, chapter by chapter.

  • Theme settings → Storm → Visitors start in sets the default: 3D flight or Regular page.
  • Visitors whose device asks for reduced motion start in page view. If they choose 3D anyway, flights land instantly and the clouds stay still.

The stops

BlockWhat it shows
Image (Release)A wide banner with a heading, text and two buttons. Leave it empty and it uses Shopify’s Home page collection image (or the first collection with an image), that collection’s title and description, then your store name and description.
IntroYour heading and text, or your store name and description.
Featured productOne product in a still circle on a white mat, its price on a tag at the bottom, with an info card beside it. When the product’s variants have their own photos, small circles show them beside the main one; choosing one swaps the photo, price, stock and link. Finish picks a variant by an option value (for example Black or 80 cm): the stop shows that variant’s photo, price and stock and links to it. An optional background image or video fills the lens behind the product.
CollectionsYour collections as a list beside a scope: choosing one locks it in, with its photo and a strip of its products. Or a cluster of hexagons.
ProductsA rack: the chosen product large on the left with its price and stock, the others on a rail to step through. Or a ring of products.
StoryOne article from your blog as a spread: its image, the date and author, the title, the opening lines and a link. Hidden until an article is chosen.
LookA shoppable photo: up to four products pinned on it as numbered hexagons, beside a panel with your words and the same products as a numbered list (photo, name, price). Pointing at a marker lights its row and the other way round. Place each marker with the two sliders under its product (across and down, in percent of the photo). Hidden until it has a photo or a product.
Closing linkThe archive: a portal to all products, with the product count.

Each block has Name in the stop menu, an optional info card, and its own angle. Reorder blocks to change the route.

More below a stop: the Image (Release) block can have a heading, text and an article Below the image, and the Featured product block a Related article. A stop with more below shows a More cue and scrolls inside itself down to it (the page itself never moves), ending with a button on to the next stop.

The Journal route

The blog page is a route too (section Journal route, the default blog template): the opening stop shows the blog’s title, article count and tags beside the newest article as a wide spread; the following stops hold three articles each, named by date in the stop menu; a last stop leads to older articles. Choose the articles per page and whether cards show the author, the opening lines and the products an article mentions. For the products, create an article metafield definition cypher.products of type List of products and pick them on each article. Article pages themselves stay flat, for reading. To use the card grid instead, replace the section with Blog in the blog template.

Background and frames, per template

Add the Background and frames section to any template to tune that page alone: the strength of the dotted cloud backdrop (and the 3D sky’s clouds) and of the glow around frames and panels. Each follows the theme setting until you switch on its override.

Lookbook template

For an editorial page: create a page (Online Store → Pages), set its template to page.lookbook, then open it in the theme editor. It starts as a route of a cover, three Look stops, a Story and a closing link; add, remove or reorder stops like on the home page. Each Look takes a photo and up to four products.

Hide stops without content (section setting, off by default) leaves out stops that have nothing to show yet, such as Collections in a store without collections. In the theme editor every stop always shows, so you can fill it.

The city and the clouds

Theme settings → Storm holds the scene:

PartSettings
ColorsSky, ground and buildings.
CityOn or off and its style: Buildings (with energy pulses in the streets and see-through buildings as options), Patches of grass, or Plants (3D), a field of low-poly plants and flowers drawn in 3D just after the page loads (fewer on phones; flat grass where 3D is unavailable). With a dark sky the plants switch to moonlight and the flowers glow. How many buildings or plants.
CloudsOn or off, digital dots (they part around the pointer) or soft clouds, how many between stops, opacity, drift speed, tone, and occasional flashes.
Regular pagesThe dotted cloud backdrop behind every other page.
Outer glowThe soft glow around frames and panels: strength, and text color or accent color. Frame lines: white or the text color.
On phonesCity, clouds and the lines between stops, each on or off.

The animations run only while they can be seen: they stop in a background tab, when the scene scrolls out of view, and for reduced-motion visitors.

Collection pages

Collection pages use the same space: the collection’s own stop first, then its products in batches. See Collections & search.
