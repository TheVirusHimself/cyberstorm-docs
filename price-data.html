Price data

Two optional pieces of information under the product price: price history, a chart of how the price changed, and units sold, a count such as “128 sold”. Both are off until you turn them on, and both stay hidden on a product until it has data.

What shoppers see

  • Price history: one sentence and a small line under the price: “Lowest price in 6 months”, “Price unchanged for 3 months” or “Lowest price in the last 30 days: …”. Opening it shows the price over 3 months, 6 months, 1 year or all, the price on the day under the pointer, and today / lowest / highest. Screen readers get a description of the chart and the list of changes.
  • Units sold: “128 sold” beside the stock status.

Turn them on

Theme editor → Product page → the Price and stock block → Price data:

SettingWhat it does
Show price historyThe sentence, line and chart.
Show units soldThe “N sold” readout.
Show fromThe smallest number that shows (default 10), so a new product does not read “2 sold”.

Where the data comes from

A theme can show store data but cannot record it: it cannot remember old prices or count sales on its own. Both numbers live in product metafields that the store keeps up to date, by hand, with Shopify Flow, with an app, or with a script that uses Shopify’s Admin API.

InformationNamespace and keyType
Price historycyber.price_historyJSON
Units soldcyber.units_soldInteger
Create both as metafield definitions first (Settings → Custom data → Products → Add definition) with exactly these namespaces, keys and types. They then appear on every product’s page in the admin, where you can fill them in.

Price history format

{
  "v": 1,
  "p": [
    ["2026-04-01", 210.00, 350.00],
    ["2026-06-15", 235.00],
    ["2026-08-20", 189.00, 350.00]
  ]
}
  • v is the format version, always 1.
  • p has one row per price change, oldest first: the day the price started (YYYY-MM-DD), the price as a number in your store currency (a dot for decimals, no currency sign), and optionally the compare-at price at the time.
  • Only the days a price changed are needed; daily rows work too, repeats are skipped. Rows dated in the future are ignored.
  • Today’s price needs no row: the theme adds the product’s current price, so the line always ends at what the shopper pays now.
  • The history appears once a product has at least one price change or 30 days of history.

Write the whole history every time. A metafield is replaced, not added to: when a price changes, save the full list with the new row added.

With an app or a script

Anything with Admin API access can write the history with the metafieldsSet mutation (owner: the product; namespace cyber; key price_history; type json; value: the JSON above as text). Up to 25 products per call; for a whole catalogue use a bulk operation.

Keeping units sold up to date

Any whole number works. To count sales automatically with Shopify Flow (free in your admin):

  1. Trigger: Order created.
  2. Add For each over the order’s line items.
  3. Action: Update product metafield, namespace cyber, key units_sold, type integer, value: the product’s current cyber.units_sold plus the line item’s quantity.
  4. Place a test order and check the product’s metafield before relying on it.

Refunds and cancellations are not subtracted unless you add a workflow for them. You can also simply update the number by hand from time to time.

Check it

  1. Fill both metafields on one product.
  2. Turn the settings on in the Price and stock block.
  3. Open the product: “N sold” beside the stock, and the line and sentence under the price.
  4. Open the price history: the chart, the period buttons and today / lowest / highest.
